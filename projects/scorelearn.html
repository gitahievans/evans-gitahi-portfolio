Music intelligence pipeline case study

Scorelearn

I built a full-stack workflow that turns sheet-music PDFs, score images, and recorded audio into one analyzable score model, then makes the result visible, playable, downloadable, and easier to understand.

Engineering brief

Scorelearn treats score analysis as a pipeline, not a single AI request. It first converts different media into symbolic music, repairs that intermediate representation, derives inspectable music-theory facts, and only then uses generation to explain those facts to a learner.

InputsPDF, JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP, MP3, WAV, and M4A
OutputsStructured analysis, MusicXML, MIDI, notation, playback, summary
SurfacesUpload, live job progress, results, score viewer, player

System boundary

The browser owns interaction and visualization, while Django owns validation and the score record. Heavy recognition and analysis leave the request cycle entirely: Celery workers branch by source type, converge on MusicXML, and persist both structured results and artifact endpoints for the frontend.

Critical decisions

Decision 01 / Architecture

Converge every source on a symbolic score

PDFs and images pass through Audiveris optical music recognition; audio passes through Basic Pitch to produce MIDI. Both branches become MusicXML before the rest of the product analyzes or presents them.

Why it matters: analysis, notation, playback, and downloads share one downstream contract instead of duplicating logic for every input type.

Decision 02 / Responsiveness

Model long-running work as an observable job

Uploads dispatch a Celery task through Redis. The worker publishes named stages and progress percentages, while React Query polls the score endpoint and stops only at a terminal state.

Why it matters: expensive recognition never holds an HTTP request open, and a failed validation, transcription, cleanup, or analysis stage remains visible to the user.

Decision 03 / Data quality

Repair the transcription before trusting its analysis

The normalization pass quantizes timing and duration, removes duplicate or overlapping note events, limits implausibly dense onsets, selects a clef, and can split piano-range material into two staves.

Tradeoff: cleanup can simplify nuance, but it produces a more stable draft for theory analysis and playback. The interface labels audio transcriptions as drafts rather than polished engraving.

Decision 04 / AI boundary

Derive musical facts before generating an explanation

Music21 detects key, meter, tempo, parts, instrumentation, chords, measures, dynamics, accidentals, and articulations. Gemini receives those results afterward to create a concise, beginner-focused explanation.

Why it matters: generative text explains an inspectable analysis payload; it is not asked to infer the score directly from an opaque upload.

Learning workspace

01

Inspect the score

OpenSheetMusicDisplay renders the generated MusicXML with responsive zoom and pan, and users can download the symbolic score for work in other notation tools.

02

Hear the result

A Tone.js MIDI player exposes transport, progress, tempo, volume, and instrument controls. Audio uploads keep the source recording available beside the generated score playback.

03

Understand the theory

Results separate primary signatures from structure and performance details, then add an on-demand explanation aimed at learners who may not yet know the terminology.

Music transcription is probabilistic: clean solo recordings and high-quality scans work better than dense mixes or damaged scores. The product communicates that boundary and presents generated notation as an editable draft, while the next hardening step is measuring accuracy across a representative test corpus.

What this demonstrates

  • Designing a heterogeneous media pipeline around a stable symbolic intermediate representation.
  • Moving CPU-heavy recognition into asynchronous workers with explicit progress, failure, and cleanup states.
  • Combining deterministic music analysis with a deliberately narrow generative explanation layer.
  • Building one responsive workspace across upload, analysis, score rendering, audio, MIDI, and downloadable artifacts.
